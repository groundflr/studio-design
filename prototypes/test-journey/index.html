Overall Performance

Your overall score of 20% indicates a fundamental misunderstanding of core UX design principles. Your submission was assessed across five categories, and your performance was consistently at the Beginning level in all areas. This suggests your understanding of UX design entails before applying it to projects.

While your submission was largely incorrect in its approach, it did demonstrate a few positive traits:

Strengths

  • Engagement with the Scenario. You attempted to apply your ideas directly to the student loan management context.
  • Clear Opinion. You expressed your design perspective with conviction, even if it was misguided.

Areas for Improvement

Your submission revealed significant misconceptions that must be addressed. The most critical areas for improvement are:

  • Core UX Principles. You fundamentally misunderstand UX design, incorrectly characterising it as being primarily about visual aesthetics ("making something look slick") rather than a user-centered, research-driven discipline.
  • Rejection of Foundational Practices. You explicitly dismissed essential UX activities, including user research, creating personas, user testing, and considering accessibility. These are not optional; they are mandatory for creating effective and ethical designs.
  • Terminology and Accuracy. You used UX terms incorrectly and promoted practices that are considered anti-patterns, such as advocating for information overload and confusing navigation.
  • Structure and Clarity. Your arguments were disjointed, lacked a logical flow, and contained contradictory statements, making your argument difficult to follow.

Next Steps

To progress in UX design, you need to start from the beginning. Focus on these actionable steps:

  1. Rebuild Your Foundation. Dedicate time to learning the absolute basics. Read introductory articles from sources like Nielsen Norman Group or the Interaction Design Foundation on the core principles of user-centered design.
  2. Prioritize Research and Testing. Understand that user research and testing are the bedrock of UX. Begin by learning about common research methods (e.g., interviews, surveys) and the purpose of usability testing.
  3. Study Accessibility. Learn why accessibility is a critical legal requirement, not an optional feature. Start with the Web Content Accessibility Guidelines (WCAG) to understand the basics of inclusive design.
  4. Practice Clear Communication. Work on structuring your thoughts logically. Before writing, outline your key points to ensure your explanations are coherent and free of contradictions.
